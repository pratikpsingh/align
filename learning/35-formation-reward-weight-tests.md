# Testing a formation-reward weight

A reward weight changes what the learner is paid for; it does not directly steer the drone. In the plane task, the **formation** term measures distortion of inter-drone distances. The **tracking** term measures distance from each assigned slot. Four drones can make a correct square in the wrong place: shape error is low while tracking error remains high. They can also reach their slots badly spaced during travel: tracking can improve while safety and temporary shape worsen.

The current [feasible-schedule run](../docs/35-feasible-schedule-training.md) gave seed 41 a lower assigned error after training, but pairwise error and near-separation got worse. Seed 73 barely improved. Its frozen actor often commanded away from targets, so we do not know whether increasing the formation penalty will help or simply trade one failure for another.

The treatment multiplies only `formation_weight` by four. For example, if a fixed drone state has raw formation score `−0.5`, `dt=0.01 s`, and baseline weight 1, the weighted contribution is `−0.005`; weight 4 changes it to `−0.020`. All other contributions on that **same fixed state** stay equal. Once the policy is retrained, the states and actions will change; that is the causal effect we must measure. `tests/test_formation_reward_ablation.py` checks the one-field change and exact arithmetic.

The experiment pairs seeds and checkpoint updates, verifies both runs' raw rows, and compares success, assigned error, pairwise error, and separation. It deliberately does not compare total reward magnitude because a four-times weight changes its units of comparison. The [operational protocol](../docs/36-formation-reward-ablation.md) gives the exact run and CPU comparison commands. A passing launcher means the experiment executed correctly; only its flight metrics tell us whether the change helped.

## What the matched run showed

The weight-4 run and strict comparison passed on 2026-09-18 IST; see the [operational result](../docs/36-formation-reward-ablation.md). Each arm evaluated 24 episodes. Neither succeeded. At update 4, seed 41's assigned and pairwise errors decreased modestly with weight 4 and its minimum separation increased; seed 73's errors increased modestly and its minimum separation decreased. This is why a single attractive trajectory or a larger numerical reward is insufficient evidence: the controlled change did not consistently improve both seeds, and it did not solve the mission. The next check should examine the frozen actor's direction and speed choices under the treatment and then change one action-selection mechanism at a time.

The frozen replays give a more concrete view: seed 41 chose commands toward its target on 61.3% of formation drone steps under weight 4, versus 45.8% under weight 1, yet its mean command speed stayed near 0.05 m/s. Seed 73 remained at 25.0% target-directed commands despite a small speed increase. Both failed every evaluated episode. A reward weight can affect choices, but the physical trajectory is the acceptance test. The fourth policy action is decoded with an absolute value, so positive and negative speed coordinates can represent the same commanded speed; that redundancy should be tested as its own interface intervention if pursued.
