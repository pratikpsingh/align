# Feasible timing and training exposure

A drone policy can learn only from the situations its rollouts contain. In the accepted four-drone plane reference, formation begins after 50 ground steps and 1,500 takeoff steps. The simple target-directed controller first satisfies success at episode step 2,104. An old 768-step rollout never reaches the formation phase if every update begins with a new episode. Calling that a formation-learning experiment would be misleading even if PPO itself runs correctly.

The [feasible-schedule probe](../docs/35-feasible-schedule-training.md) sets the rollout to 2,304 steps while leaving the episode deadline at 2,350. One update can therefore collect about 754 formation steps after its first 1,550 ground/takeoff steps. The last 46 episode steps remain outside that rollout unless the environment terminates and resets sooner. The learner still bootstraps an unfinished rollout and resets the simulator between updates according to its existing contract; it does not pretend that flight state is resumed from a checkpoint.

This is a protocol choice, not a proof of learning. A random actor may crash, separate, or reset before formation, so the *observed* phase rows matter more than the planned 754-step maximum. Each seed gets four PPO updates and frozen evaluations at updates 0, 2, and 4. A correct result reports the number of formation samples, success and failure reasons, position and shape error, and safety; a reward curve alone cannot show mission completion. CPU tests check that the explicit construction, task, rollout, and evaluation files match the accepted reference timing and each other. The accepted GPU result is summarized below.

## What four updates actually showed

The accepted GPU run is `20260918T172025.554059IST-f5bd6176`. It verifies that each seed saw 3,016 formation environment rows per update and that all 24 frozen evaluation episodes reached formation. None succeeded. For seed 41, whole-episode assigned error improved, but its final formation pairwise error grew from roughly 0.99 m to 1.13 m during the phase; every formation step fell below the 0.55 m shaping margin, though the 0.25 m terminal threshold was not reached. Seed 73 showed little formation improvement. A value network can fit returns increasingly well while the actor still flies poorly, so positive explained variance is not a success metric.

This result rules out *absence of formation samples* in this new protocol. It does not identify the exact cause of weak learning. Per-drone telemetry is needed to see whether the frozen actor points toward its target, how the inner controller follows that command, and which weighted reward term dominates at formation time. The raw values and operational limits are in [the training protocol](../docs/35-feasible-schedule-training.md).

Both final checkpoints were then replayed with per-drone telemetry. Seed 41 commanded only 0.055 m/s on average during formation and pointed toward its target in 45.8% of rows; seed 73 commanded 0.013 m/s and pointed toward target in 25.0%. Their realized speeds were similarly small. This helps locate the failure upstream of the inner velocity controller for these checkpoints: the learned actor requested weak or misdirected motion. It does **not** prove which reward or optimizer setting caused those requests. See the accepted [raw replay diagnosis](../docs/35-feasible-schedule-training.md).
