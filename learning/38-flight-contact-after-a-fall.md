# Why a fallen drone must still count as a contact failure

A drone begins on the floor, so the task cannot treat every floor contact as a crash. The old rule waited until the drone's center was above 0.25 m before it counted a measured contact force. That works during takeoff, but creates a blind spot later: if a drone falls back to 0.06 m while trying to hold a formation, it can sit on the floor with a nonzero force and the task may report only a timeout.

The scheduled phase supplies the missing context. During ground-settle, floor contact is expected. During low takeoff, it remains allowed until the center rises above the height threshold. During formation, the drone is supposed to be flying, so a force above 0.01 N is unsafe regardless of its current height. The new guard is `contact_above_threshold AND (above_takeoff_height OR in_formation_phase)`. It uses the existing physical contact measurement; it does not guess a collision from position alone. A force reading may come from the ground or another object on the tracked base link, so the raw trajectory is still needed to identify what happened.

For example, give a drone at z=0.06 m a 0.02 N measured contact. In ground or low takeoff the episode continues. In formation it ends with `flight_contact` (numeric reason 3), and the contact reward term is active. The CPU task test proves this phase distinction. In the already saved failed flights, the first newly guarded event would be at step 2,981 or 3,044, depending on the narrow-pyramid configuration, with about 0.5 N of contact force at z≈0.06 m. Reclassifying old CSV rows demonstrates why the former timeouts were misleading, but it cannot substitute for a new simulator run with the corrected code. The simulator uses the same Boolean expression on tensors of shape `[world, drone]`. The rebuilt-image falling-drone run below has now checked it physically. Old runs preserve their earlier meanings; they must not be retroactively relabeled.

Code: [CPU task](../src/align/tasks/environment.py), [simulator task](../src/align/simulation/vector_task.py), and [test](../tests/test_task_environment.py). The [operational guide](../docs/39-flight-contact-termination.md) explains the acceptance check.

The physical regression reached the predicted failure point. At step 3,043, one low drone still had no measured contact and the episode continued. One step later, its base link measured about 0.516 N at z≈0.060 m, and the episode ended as `flight_contact`. The old height-only rule would miss that contact because 0.060 m is below 0.25 m. Conversely, a force of about 0.066 N during step 1 on the floor was allowed because the task was still in its ground phase. This paired observation shows why both measured force **and** flight phase are needed; neither alone expresses the intended safety rule.
