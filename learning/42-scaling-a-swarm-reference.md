# What changes when a swarm grows to eight drones

A shared actor can run the same 55-number local observation for each drone: its own velocity and target error plus at most seven nearby drones and masks. Adding drones does not change this per-drone input width. The centralized training critic is different: it stores a fixed set of global agent slots, currently eight. Eight drones fill that capacity; a ninth would need a different critic design or an actor-only evaluator for frozen-policy flight.

Geometry changes even when network dimensions do not. Four drones on a 1 m-spaced launch line reach only ±1.5 m. Eight reach ±3.5 m. The eight-drone plane slots remain near the center, so the farthest drone moves about 2.74 m after takeoff. At a 0.5 m/s limit it needs at least 5.48 s just to cover that distance; slowing near the target and holding formation take more time. The explicit reference config therefore grants 12 s for formation rather than the four-drone 8 s.

We can calculate a nominal safety check before flying: if every drone moved simultaneously along the straight line from its takeoff point to its assigned slot, the closest pair would stay 0.707 m apart. The task's planned margin is 0.55 m. This does not guarantee physical clearance because real drones accelerate, drift, and interact. The [CPU checks](../tests/test_eight_drone_reference.py) validate geometry, deadline, and input shapes. The [operational guide](../docs/43-eight-drone-reference.md) gives the one-world physical command and accepted measurements. The physical reference completed two repeat episodes successfully, each after 14.59 s. Its smallest measured separation after ground departure was 0.663 m, below the ideal straight-line prediction of 0.707 m but still above the 0.55 m planning margin. Final-dwell assigned error stayed under 0.0231 m, and guarded airborne contact force was zero. This is a useful lesson: ideal geometry is a planning screen, while the trajectory is the actual safety observation. The controller knew every drone's exact target and pose; the result establishes task feasibility at eight, not learned-policy generalization.
